Wine Cellar Workbook — Roadmap

Where the cellar tool stands, and where it can go

The workbook is live and ready for day-to-day use. Below is a short outline of what's built, and a set of options for what we could add next as the collection grows.

Currently in place

A full catalogue for roughly 1,200 bottles — label photo, producer, vintage, region, drinking window, critic scores, and storage location — with dropdown validation to keep entries tidy, a dashboard that automatically surfaces what's ready to drink, what's coming into its window next year, and what's out of stock, and a quick pop-up form for adding new bottles without touching the spreadsheet directly.

Proposed next steps

Ordered by how directly each builds on the last.

1
Everyday usability Quick win

Small additions that remove manual upkeep from the routine you already have.

Consumption log
A "drink a bottle" action that updates the count and quietly keeps a history of what was opened and when.
Window reminders
A monthly note by email listing what's entering or leaving its drinking window — no need to check the sheet.
2
Capture at the point of purchase

Logging a bottle the moment it arrives, rather than catching up later.

Mobile entry
A phone-friendly form to log a new bottle and snap its label photo on the spot.
Shelf labels
A printable code per bottle that opens straight to its record — handy for a quick shelf check.
3
Richer data, less typing

Once the basics are second nature, the sheet can start doing more of the work itself.

Critic score lookup
Pull published scores automatically by producer and vintage, where available.
Value tracking
A running estimate of the cellar's current worth, not just what was originally paid.

A place to start

I'd suggest beginning with the consumption log — it's the fastest to add, immediately useful, and sets up mobile entry naturally as a follow-on.

Happy to scope any of these individually, or set a quarterly check-in to keep things running smoothly as the collection grows.
