Wine Cellar Workbook — How to Use It

A quick guide to your cellar sheet

Nothing here requires spreadsheet experience. This walks through everything you need for day-to-day use — adding a bottle, checking what's ready to drink, and finding a wine again later.

Open your Wine Cellar Sheet →

Three tabs, one purpose each

You'll see these along the bottom of the screen.

Dashboard

Your at-a-glance view — what's ready to drink, what's coming up next year, and what's run out.

Cellar

The full list of every bottle you own. This is where new entries land and where you'll browse or search.

Read Me

Reference notes about how the sheet is organized, kept for whenever you need a reminder.

Adding a new bottle

No typing into the grid required — use the built-in form.

  1. At the top of the screen, click the 🍷 Cellar menu (next to Help).

  2. Choose Add Bottle… — a form pops up with a field for every detail: producer, vintage, region, quantity, drinking window, critic scores, and so on.

  3. Fill in what you know. Only Producer and Wine Name are required — leave anything else blank and fill it in later.

  4. Click Add to cellar. The bottle appears at the bottom of the Cellar tab, already dated and numbered — nothing else to do.

Pasting a review? Drop the full text into Reviews & Notes as-is, then put just the number in the matching score column (Halliday, RP, My Score, etc.) so you can sort by it later.

Adding a label photo

  1. Find or take a photo of the label and get a web link to it — the easiest way is uploading it to Google Drive, right-clicking the file, and choosing Share ▸ Copy link (set it to "Anyone with the link").

  2. Paste that link into the Label Photo (URL) column, right next to the Photo column.

  3. The photo appears automatically in the very first column — no need to resize or format anything.

Reading the Drink Status colors

Each row is color-coded so you can tell a bottle's status without reading every column.

Ready Drink now Too young Past peak Out of stock
This updates itself — you never set it directly. It's calculated from today's date, the drinking window, and how many bottles are left.

Finding a bottle again

  1. On the Cellar tab, click any column header — a small arrow appears.

  2. Click it to sort (e.g. by rating, highest first) or filter (e.g. show only Red wines, or only bottles from a certain producer).

  3. To search by name, press Ctrl+F (or ⌘+F on a Mac) and type what you're looking for.

That covers everyday use. You shouldn't need to touch anything beyond the 🍷 Cellar menu and the Cellar tab itself for normal day-to-day updates.
